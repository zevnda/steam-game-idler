Steam trading cards are collectible items you earn by playing games that support them. Each game
drops about half of its card set while you play. You collect the rest by trading, buying from the
Community Market, or opening booster packs. A complete set can be crafted into a badge that gives
you 100 XP toward your Steam level, plus a profile background and an emoticon. Any card you don't
want can be sold on the Community Market for Steam Wallet funds.

<KeyTakeaways>
- Cards drop from playtime, not achievements. Most games drop about half their set.
- Once a game's drops run out, you can still get random booster packs of 3 cards.
- Crafting a full set gives a badge, 100 XP, a profile background and an emoticon. You can craft
  the same badge up to 5 levels.
- Cards sell on the Community Market. Steam takes a 5% fee and the game's publisher takes a
  game-specific fee on top.
- Limited accounts (under $5 USD spent on Steam) don't get trading cards at all.
</KeyTakeaways>

## How card drops work

You earn card drops by playing a game that has trading cards. According to Valve's
[Steam Trading Cards FAQ](https://steamcommunity.com/tradingcards/faq), "most games will drop a
number of cards equal to about half the number of cards in that game's set." A game with an
8-card set drops about 4 cards, so you can never complete a set from drops alone.

A few rules shape when and how cards drop:

- **Drops come from playtime, not achievements.** Unlocking achievements doesn't earn cards.
- **Free-to-play games work differently.** Games like Team Fortress 2 and Dota 2 drop roughly one
  card for every $9 USD spent in the game, instead of from playtime.
- **New games may not drop cards right away.** Since May 2017, Valve has held back drops for new
  games until they pass a
  ["confidence metric"](https://steamcommunity.com/games/593110/announcements/detail/1954971077935370845)
  showing that real players are buying and playing them. When a game passes it, cards drop for
  playtime you'd already logged. Valve added this to fight "fake" games that bot accounts idled
  only to farm cards.
- **Your account must not be limited.** Steam's
  [Limited User Accounts](https://help.steampowered.com/en/faqs/view/71D3-35C2-AD96-AA3A) page
  lists "Gaining Steam Profile Levels (Locked to level 0) and Trading Cards" among the restricted
  features. Spending at least $5 USD on Steam lifts that restriction.

You can see how many drops a game has left on its badge page under your profile's **Badges**.
If cards stop dropping when you expect them, our
[card drop times guide](/docs/features/card-farming/card-drop-times) covers the usual causes.

## Booster packs

Booster packs are how you keep getting cards after a game's drops run out. Valve's FAQ explains
that once you've received all of a game's card drops, you become eligible for booster packs. Each
pack contains 3 cards, which may include foils.

Booster packs aren't guaranteed. Steam grants them randomly to eligible players as other people in
the community craft badges. To stay eligible you need to sign in to Steam at least once a week.
Your Steam level also raises your chances: +20% at level 10, rising by 20 percentage points every 10
levels to +100% at level 50.

## Crafting badges

A badge is what you get for completing a game's card set. Crafting one uses up the cards in the set
and gives you:

- The game's badge on your profile
- 100 XP toward your Steam level
- A random emoticon and a random profile background from that game
- A chance at a discount coupon for another game or DLC

You can craft the same game's badge up to five times. Each extra craft needs another full set,
gives another 100 XP, and raises the badge's level. Your **Badges** page shows your progress on
every set, which friends have the cards you're missing, and a shortcut to trade with them.

## Steam levels and XP

Your Steam level goes up as you earn XP, and crafting badges is the main way to earn it. Each level
takes 100 XP at first, and the XP needed per level goes up every 10 levels: 200 after level 10, then
300 after level 20.

Leveling up has some real benefits on Steam:

- Your friends list limit goes up by 5 every level.
- You unlock an extra profile showcase every 10 levels.
- Your chance of booster pack drops goes up, as described above.

Years of Service and Steam Community badges also give XP, but for most people a higher Steam
level means crafting a lot of trading card badges.

## Foil cards

Every card has a small chance of dropping as a foil, a rarer version with a shiny border. A full
set of foil cards crafts into a foil badge. Valve's FAQ says foil badges carry no extra benefit beyond rarity.

## Selling and trading cards

Cards are ordinary Steam inventory items, so you can trade them with friends or sell them on the
**Steam Community Market**. This is how many players turn cards from games they don't care about
into Steam Wallet funds to put toward new games. Steam's Community Market FAQ lists two fees on a
sale:

| Fee | Who sets it | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Steam Transaction Fee | Steam | 5% (minimum $0.01) |
| Game-specific fee | The game's publisher | 10% for Valve's own games, set per publisher |

Both fees are charged on top of the price you receive, so the buyer pays more than you get. Our
[marketplace fees page](/docs/features/inventory-manager/marketplace-fees) shows what you'll
actually take home. Trades are final, as Valve's FAQ puts it: "make sure you really want to make a
trade before confirming." Limited accounts can't use the Market or trade at all.

## How Steam Game Idler fits in

Card drops only need a game to be running on your account, which is why idling tools exist.
[Steam Game Idler's Card Farming](/docs/features/card-farming) works through every game in your
library that still has drops left, without you downloading or playing them. For accounts that need
some playtime before cards start dropping, it builds that playtime in batches of up to 32 games
first, then farms each game until its drops are used up. Our
[how card farming works](/docs/features/card-farming/how-it-works) page explains the details.

When the cards are in your inventory, the [Inventory Manager](/docs/features/inventory-manager) can
list them on the Community Market. It accounts for the fees for you: the price you enter is the
price the buyer pays.

Both features are free. [Download Steam Game Idler](/download) to see how many card drops your
library still has.

## FAQ

### How many trading cards can you get from one game?

Most games drop about half of their card set, according to Valve. A game with 10 cards in its set
drops about 5. The rest come from trading, the Community Market or booster packs. Free-to-play
games drop cards based on in-game spending instead.

### Do you get trading cards for free games?

Free-to-play games such as Team Fortress 2 and Dota 2 drop cards based on money spent in the game,
roughly one card per $9 USD, not on playtime. Your account also has to be out of Limited status
(at least $5 USD spent on Steam) to receive cards at all.

### Why am I not getting Steam trading cards?

The usual causes are a limited account, a game with no drops left, a new game that hasn't passed
Valve's confidence metric yet, or not enough playtime yet. Our
[card drop times guide](/docs/features/card-farming/card-drop-times) goes through each one.

### What do you get for crafting a Steam badge?

You get the badge, 100 XP, a random emoticon and profile background from that game, and a chance
at a discount coupon. You can craft each game's badge up to five times.

### Are Steam trading cards worth any money?

They can be sold on the Steam Community Market for Steam Wallet funds, which you can spend on
games and items on Steam. Prices vary by game and card, so check the Market listing for the cards
you have. Steam's 5% fee and the publisher's fee are charged on every sale.

### Do achievements give trading cards?

No. Valve's FAQ is clear that card drops come from playtime, not achievements.
