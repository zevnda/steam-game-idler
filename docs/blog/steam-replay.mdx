Steam Replay is Valve's yearly recap of what you played on Steam. It counts your online playtime
from the first second of January 1 to the last second of December 14 (GMT), and leaves out
Offline Mode, games marked as Private, tools and unreleased games. Valve usually releases it in
mid-December. Steam Replay 2026 hasn't been announced yet, and nothing played after December 14
makes it into that year's Replay.

<KeyTakeaways>
- Steam Replay covers playtime from January 1 to December 14 (GMT). Play from December 15 to 31
  falls outside every year's window.
- Offline Mode play, games marked as Private, tools and non-game software, and unreleased,
  preloaded or prerelease games are all left out.
- Replay counts the same playtime as your library, so AFK and idled hours show up too.
- Adult members of your Steam Family can see your Replay automatically. Everyone else sees it
  only if you set it to Friends Only or Public.
- Viewing your Replay earns a badge worth 50 XP each year.
</KeyTakeaways>

## What is Steam Replay?

Steam Replay is a personal page that sums up your year on Steam: your most-played games, total
hours, sessions, achievements, your longest daily streak and how your playtime split across
months and devices. Valve
[launched it in 2022](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/593110/announcements/detail/3646257819398517213),
renamed it "Steam Year in Review" for 2023, and
[went back to Steam Replay in 2024](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/593110/announcements/detail/641297020996289524),
when it added "playtime by month; playtime by what you played on; even playtime across your Steam
Family."

You'll find it at [store.steampowered.com/replay](https://store.steampowered.com/replay/) once
it's live. Valve also emails a link and adds Replay to the drop-down menu under your username in
Steam, according to its
[2023 announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/593110/announcements/detail/3877100446784043484).
You can switch between years on the page.

## When does Steam Replay come out?

Valve has released Steam Replay in mid-December every year. The 2023 and 2024 editions both went
live on December 18, going by Valve's announcements, and GamingOnLinux
[reported the 2025 edition live](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/12/steam-replay-is-live-and-notes-only-14-of-playtime-spent-by-all-steam-users-was-for-2025-releases/)
on December 17, 2025. As of November 16, 2026, Valve hasn't announced a date for Steam Replay
2026, and Replay isn't on Steamworks'
[upcoming events list](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events). The Steam
Winter Sale starts on December 17, 2026, and Replay has landed within a day or two of the Winter
Sale before.

## When does Steam Replay stop counting?

Steam Replay stops counting at the end of December 14. The FAQ on Valve's Replay page says Steam
Replay "includes all of your play time between the first second of January 1 and the last second
of December 14 (GMT)."

Two details trip people up:

- **The cutoff is GMT, not your local time.** In the Americas, December 14 GMT ends on the
  afternoon or evening of December 14. In much of Asia and Oceania, it ends on December 15 local
  time.
- **The last two weeks of December aren't counted anywhere.** Each Replay starts on January 1, so
  by Valve's own wording, playtime from December 15 to 31 isn't in that year's Replay or the next
  one.

## What Steam Replay counts and leaves out

Steam Replay uses the same playtime Steam records for your library, with a few exclusions. Every
"Not counted" row below comes from the FAQ on Valve's Replay page.

| Playtime | In your Replay? | What Valve says |
|---|---|---|
| Online play in a released game | Yes | "all of your play time" in the date window |
| AFK, paused or idled time | Yes | Steam records playtime while a game runs ([how Steam tracks playtime](/blog/how-steam-tracks-playtime)) |
| Offline Mode | No | "does not include any playtime played in offline mode or when disconnected from internet" |
| Games marked as Private | No | "Games marked as Private are not included and will not appear in your Replay" |
| Tools and non-game software | No | "excludes time spent running tools and other types of non-game software" |
| Unreleased, preload or prerelease games | No | also excludes games "that are unreleased, in preload, in prelease, or disabled" |
| Play after December 14 (GMT) | No | outside the date window |

Replay also splits your playtime by input. Valve defines "Keyboard + Mouse" as "playtime on PC,
Mac, or Linux without an active Controller being used," and "Controller" as "playtime with an
active Controller being used, or playtime on a Steam Deck."

### Offline Mode time

Offline Mode play never reaches Replay. That's clearer than Steam's own
[Offline Mode FAQ](https://help.steampowered.com/en/faqs/view/0E18-319B-E34B-B2C8), which doesn't
mention playtime at all. If you play a lot with Steam set to Offline Mode, or on a PC or Steam
Deck without internet, expect those games to look smaller in your Replay than in your library.

### Private games

A game you've marked as Private doesn't appear in your Replay. The Replay page's text holds two
versions of this answer, and one is more specific about timing: Replay "excludes any app marked
as private as of December 14th." So a game's privacy at the cutoff is what decides it.
Valve doesn't say whether changing a game's privacy after December 14 changes a Replay that's
already been made. Steam Community threads report mixed results, so set privacy before the
cutoff.

To mark a game as private, right-click it in your Steam library and open **Manage**. Private
games have side effects: Steam
[hides the game's ownership, playtime and activity](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/593110/announcements/detail/3901870709962551196)
from everyone else, and the game doesn't drop trading cards while it's private, as we covered in
[how to hide Steam achievements](/blog/remove-steam-achievements).

## Why is a game missing from my Steam Replay?

When a game you played isn't in your Replay, or shows fewer hours than you expected, it's usually
one of these:

1. **You played it after December 14 (GMT).** That time is outside the window.
2. **You played it in Offline Mode** or without internet.
3. **It's marked as Private** in your library.
4. **It's a tool, a demo of an unreleased game, or a prerelease build.** Valve excludes
   "unreleased, in preload, in prelease, or disabled" games.
5. **The account had no playtime in the year.** Replay then shows "Sorry, this account does not
   have any playtime this year."

If none of these fit, Steam Community threads from past years report occasional gaps that Valve
never explained. There's no setting to rebuild a Replay.

## Who can see your Steam Replay?

Your Replay page has its own visibility setting: **Private**, **Friends Only** or **Public**. A
link only works for other people once you choose Friends Only or Public. Valve's FAQ lists three
ways to share it: share the page's URL, share a summary image (in three sizes for different
social sites), or add a Steam Replay showcase to your profile.

There's one exception to keeping it private. Valve's FAQ says "all adult members in a Steam Family
can automatically access the Steam Replay for other members of the Steam Family. Child members do
not have any special access privilege." If you're in a Steam Family, the adults in it can see your
Replay whatever its visibility is set to.

## The Steam Replay badge

Opening your Replay earns a yearly badge worth 50 XP. The page shows an **Add Badge To Profile**
button. It's one of the few free sources of Steam XP, along with Years of Service and the Steam
Awards. [How to level up on Steam](/blog/how-to-level-up-on-steam) lists the others.

## What this means for Steam Game Idler

Hours you idle with Steam Game Idler count in your Steam Replay like any other playtime. The
[Playtime Booster](/docs/features/playtime-booster), card farming and the achievement unlocker all
work by telling Steam a game is running. With Steam Sign-in, the app announces up to 32 games at
once straight to Steam's servers. With Local Sign-in, it runs one small process per game through
your own Steam client. Either way Steam records ordinary online playtime, and Valve's FAQ lists no
exception for it.

So if you idle a lot, expect it to shape your Replay: idled games in your top games, more total
hours, and possibly a longer daily streak. If you'd rather keep a game out, mark it as Private
before the December 14 cutoff. Keep the trade-off in mind: a private game doesn't drop trading
cards, so it's a poor choice for a game you're
[farming cards on](/docs/features/card-farming/card-drop-times). A private game can also be
missing from Steam Game Idler's own games list, because the app reads your library the way other
tools do.

To limit how much time a game gets in the first place, use the **Max playtime** or **Max idle
time** setting on that game. And remember the cutoff: anything you idle from December 15 to 31 is
outside every Replay. [Download Steam Game Idler](/download) to try the Playtime Booster.

## FAQ

### When does Steam Replay 2026 come out?

Valve hasn't announced it yet. Past editions went live on December 18 in 2023 and 2024, and
around December 16 to 17 in 2025, so mid-December 2026 is likely but not confirmed.

### When does Steam Replay stop tracking?

At the last second of December 14 (GMT). Valve's Replay FAQ says it "includes all of your play
time between the first second of January 1 and the last second of December 14 (GMT)."

### Does Steam Replay count December?

Only December 1 to 14. Playtime from December 15 to 31 isn't in that year's Replay, and the next
year's Replay starts on January 1.

### Does Steam Replay count offline hours?

No. Valve's FAQ says Steam Replay "does not include any playtime played in offline mode or when
disconnected from internet."

### Does Steam Replay show private games?

No. Games marked as Private "are not included and will not appear in your Replay," according to
Valve's FAQ. What matters is whether the game is private on December 14.

### How do I make my Steam Replay private?

Set the page's visibility to Private at the top of your Replay. Adult members of your Steam
Family can still see it, because Valve gives them access automatically.

### How do I get the Steam Replay badge?

Open your Steam Replay once it's live and use the Add Badge To Profile button. The badge is worth
50 XP.

### Do idled hours show up in Steam Replay?

Yes. Idling tools tell Steam a game is running, and Steam records that as normal online
playtime, the same playtime Replay sums up. Mark a game as Private before December 14 to keep it
out, but it won't drop trading cards while it's private.
